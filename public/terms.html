ATHLY is not medical advice

ATHLY is general nutrition guidance, not medical or dietetic advice. It does not diagnose anything, it does not treat anything, and it is not a substitute for a doctor or a registered dietitian who knows you.

The app calculates targets from equations and reference intakes that are published openly, and it shows you which ones. Those equations were validated on adults, not on growing teenagers, and they do not account for the energy cost of growth. No registered dietitian has yet reviewed the numbers this app produces. We say so here rather than let the confidence of a large number on a screen imply otherwise.

Talk to a doctor or a dietitian before making a real change to what you eat — and always if you have a medical condition, take medication, are pregnant, or are being treated for anything at all.

Eating, and when to stop using this

Counting calories does not suit everyone, and for some people it does harm. Do not use ATHLY if you have an eating disorder, are recovering from one, or have been told by a professional to stop tracking what you eat.

If tracking is making you anxious, making you eat less than you are hungry for, or making you feel badly about yourself, stop using the app and talk to someone — a parent, a coach, a doctor. In the US, the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline is 1-866-662-1235. Elsewhere, your doctor is the right first call. That matters more than any number in here.

Allergies — read the label anyway

ATHLY filters suggestions by the allergies you tell it about, and you must still check every label yourself. The filter is a convenience. It is not a safety system, and it cannot be treated as one.

Three reasons, all of them real:

  • It only knows the allergies you entered. Anything you did not tell it, it cannot filter.
  • Ingredient information for packaged food comes from Open Food Facts, which is contributed by members of the public and may be wrong, incomplete or out of date.
  • Recipes change, manufacturers reformulate, and food is prepared in kitchens where other food is prepared.

If an allergy of yours is serious, treat this app as a suggestion and the packet as the truth.

The numbers, and what they are worth

Targets are calculated. Food nutrition is data, and where it comes from decides how much it is worth:

  • Recipe nutrition is an authored estimate, summed from an ingredient table we maintain. It has better provenance than a hand-typed figure and it is not laboratory analysis.
  • Barcode nutrition comes from Open Food Facts, contributed by the public. We refuse rows that are physically impossible, which catches obvious errors and not subtle ones.
  • Anything you type in is exactly as accurate as you typed it.

Portions vary, cooking varies, bodies vary. Treat every figure in ATHLY as a good estimate rather than a measurement, and do not use it for anything that requires a measurement.
